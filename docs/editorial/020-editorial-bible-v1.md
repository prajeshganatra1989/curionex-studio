## 1. Why Curionex Exists

### Mission

Explain fascinating topics with clarity, curiosity, and credibility.

### Vision

Become the most trusted short-form science channel on Earth—not the loudest.

### Purpose

Curionex exists to turn everyday wonder into lasting understanding.

We take questions people already ask—

- *Why can't I remember being a baby?*
- *Why does time feel faster as I get older?*
- *What happens inside a black hole?*

—and answer them with enough honesty that the viewer walks away changed, not merely entertained.

### Why we are different

| Format | What it optimizes for | What Curionex refuses |
|--------|------------------------|------------------------|
| **Clickbait science** | Shock, fear, false mystery | Manufactured panic; fake certainty |
| **Fact channels** | Rapid trivia density | Facts without meaning or structure |
| **Wikipedia videos** | Completeness | Completeness over clarity; lecture tone |
| **AI spam** | Volume and templates | Generic voice; invented numbers; hollow wonder |

Curionex optimizes for **one unforgettable idea**, delivered with **scientific integrity** and **emotional intelligence**.

### Educational philosophy

> **Wonder over spectacle. Evidence before certainty. One idea that sticks.**

We teach the way a great National Geographic narrator would teach a friend—not the way a textbook teaches a class, and not the way a thumbnail teaches a click.

We believe:

1. Curiosity is a form of respect.
2. Uncertainty, stated clearly, builds trust.
3. The ending should change how you see the world—not summarize what you just heard.
4. Short form is not shallow form. Constraint is a craft.

### Platform note

In Curionex Studio, editorial truth is versioned as the active **Content Standard** and enforced through Knowledge Packs, Discovery Briefs, Story Spines, Master Scripts, and Quality Reviews. This Bible is the human-readable constitution those systems serve.

Related: [007-curionex-content-standard.md](./007-curionex-content-standard.md)

## 2. The Curionex Promise

Every Short must leave the viewer with at least one of these feelings:

| Promise feeling | What it means |
|-----------------|---------------|
| **"I never knew that."** | A genuine gap closed with honest science |
| **"I'll never look at this the same way."** | Perspective shift landed |
| **"Science is amazing."** | Wonder earned—not hyped |
| **"The universe still has mysteries."** | Integrity about unknowns (when true) |

### What we are *not* promising

| Anti-promise | Why we reject it |
|--------------|------------------|
| **"I got shocked."** | Shock without understanding is empty |
| **"I got tricked."** | Clickbait breaks trust permanently |
| **"I feel smarter because I won an argument."** | We are not debate bait |
| **"I need to change my life by Friday."** | We are not self-help |

### The Founding Collection test

If a finished script cannot sit beside GS-001 through GS-010 without lowering the room, it is not ready.

## 3. Target Audience

| Dimension | Definition |
|-----------|------------|
| **Primary** | Curious general viewers, roughly **13+**, English-speaking |
| **Secondary** | Adults who love science explainers; teachers; lifelong learners; parents watching with teens |
| **Reading / listening level** | Clear spoken English; everyday vocabulary; no assumed degree |
| **Attention span** | **55–60 seconds** of focused retention; every line must earn its place |
| **Curiosity level** | High. They want *why*—not a list of facts |
| **Relationship to science** | Interested, not expert; respectful of uncertainty |

### Topics to prefer

- Evergreen questions with durable wonder
- Phenomena viewers already experience (blinks, yawns, dreams, time)
- Cosmic / Earth / body / tech questions that invite a perspective shift
- Contested topics **only** when we can separate evidence, hypotheses, and unknowns cleanly

### Topics to avoid (or handle only with extreme care)

- Medical advice, diagnoses, treatment claims
- Conspiracy framing or “they don’t want you to know”
- Pseudoscience presented as open debate
- Tragedy tourism / disaster porn
- Culture-war bait disguised as science
- Topics where we cannot source authoritative references

### Brand fit filter

Ask: *Would a calm, curious friend send this to someone they respect?*  
If the answer depends on a shocking thumbnail, kill it.

## 4. Brand Voice

Canonical attributes also live on the active Content Standard. This chapter is the fuller editorial reading.

Related: [008-brand-voice.md](./008-brand-voice.md)

### Voice attributes

| Attribute | Meaning in practice |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Friendly** | Approachable; never talks down |
| **Calm** | Steady pace; no panic narration |
| **Curious** | Shared wonder—“we’re figuring this out together” |
| **Trustworthy** | Labels uncertainty; prefers primary sources |
| **Evidence-first** | Claims follow evidence, not the other way around |
| **Conversational** | Written for the ear, not the page |
| **Reflective** (when earned) | Especially finales and human topics—never preachy |

### Never

- Arrogant (“Obviously everyone knows…”)
- Sensational (“This will blow your mind!!!”)
- Robotic or academic
- Fake-intimate (“Hey bestie, let’s talk black holes”)
- Motivational-coach (“So go live your best life”)

### GOOD examples (Founding Collection)

> “Think back to this year. Where did it go?” — GS-010

> “There is a boundary in space where even light can't turn around.” — GS-005

> “Your eyelids shut for a split second—thousands of times a day—and somehow, the world still looks continuous.” — GS-009

> “But dinosaurs never completely disappeared. The next bird outside your window is still part of that ancient family—” — GS-008

### BAD examples (never ship)

> “Scientists HATE this one weird fact about black holes…”

> “Your brain is LYING to you every time you blink!!!”

> “In conclusion, yawning is caused by multiple hypothesized physiological mechanisms including thermoregulation…”

> “Time flies when you’re having fun—so seize the day!”

### CTA voice

Approved patterns:

- Follow for more fascinating facts about how the universe works.
- Follow for more fascinating facts about how your body works.
- Follow for more fascinating facts about how your mind works.

Keep CTAs light. The perspective shift does the heavy lifting—not the ask.

## 5. Script Structure

Standard flow (aligns with Content Standard story structure):

```text
Hook
  ↓
Question / Setup (curiosity gap)
  ↓
Explanation (known science)
  ↓
Twist (scientific or perspective)
  ↓
Perspective Shift (permanent reframe)
  ↓
CTA
```

| Stage | Job | Failure mode |
|-------|-----|--------------|
| **Hook** | Create honest curiosity in the first breath | Clickbait; delayed promise |
| **Question / Setup** | Make the promise clear within ~5 seconds | Vague topic wandering |
| **Explanation** | Deliver the one idea with evidence-aware clarity | Lecture; jargon pile-up |
| **Twist** | Reframe what the viewer thought they knew | Cheap shock; trivia dump |
| **Perspective Shift** | Change how they see the world afterward | Summary ending; moralizing |
| **CTA** | Soft invitation to stay | Hard sell; breaking the spell |

### Production workflow (Studio)

Every Gold script is produced through:

1. **Knowledge Pack** — research, facts, sources, angle  
2. **Discovery Brief** — viewer question, promise, takeaway, duration  
3. **Story Spine** — beats, risks, claims to verify  
4. **Master Script** — spoken narration  
5. **Editorial Quality Review** — scored gate  

Do not skip stages for “simple” topics. Simplicity is where integrity fails first.

### Stage deep-dive

#### Hook

Open on a felt moment, boundary, or incomplete story. The viewer should know what the Short is *about* before the first mid-roll of attention ends.

#### Question / Setup

State or imply the viewer question. In Discovery Briefs this is explicit; in Master Scripts it may be woven (“So why do we yawn?”) near the close—but the *promise* must appear early.

#### Explanation

Deliver established science first. Introduce leading hypotheses only after the ground is firm. Use myths as mid-course corrections, not openings—unless the myth *is* the cultural hook (then puncture it quickly).

#### Twist

Upgrade the model. Prefer scientific or perspective twists. If you cannot name the twist in one line in the Story Spine, you do not have one yet.

#### Perspective Shift

Leave an afterimage in ordinary life. See chapter 9.

#### CTA

Whisper, don’t shout. Match domain: universe / body / mind.

### Studio artifact quality bar

| Artifact | Must answer |
|----------|-------------|
| Knowledge Pack | What is known / hypothesized / unknown—and from where? |
| Discovery Brief | What does the viewer leave believing? |
| Story Spine | What is the beat-by-beat journey and risk? |
| Master Script | What is said aloud in 55–60s? |
| Quality Review | Does it meet Gold? |

## 6. The Hook System

Hooks from the Founding Collection cluster into durable patterns. Use them deliberately—do not copy wording.

### Pattern A — Personal vanishing moment

| | |
|--|--|
| **Purpose** | Instant recognition; emotional entry |
| **Psychology** | The viewer supplies their own memory |
| **When to use** | Human / mind / life-scale topics |
| **Example** | “Think back to this year. Where did it go?” (GS-010) |
| **When NOT to use** | Pure physical mechanisms with no lived experience (e.g. Wi‑Fi internals) |

### Pattern B — Invisible everyday action

| | |
|--|--|
| **Purpose** | Make the ordinary strange |
| **Psychology** | Surprise that something automatic hides complexity |
| **When to use** | Body / perception topics (blink, yawn, dream) |
| **Example** | “Your eyelids shut for a split second—thousands of times a day…” (GS-009) |
| **When NOT to use** | When the action isn’t truly common or automatic |

### Pattern C — Contactless / impossible-feeling force

| | |
|--|--|
| **Purpose** | Embodied physics curiosity |
| **Psychology** | Conflict between intuition and observation |
| **When to use** | Forces, fields, everyday physics |
| **Example** | Paperclip drifting closer across empty air (GS-007) |
| **When NOT to use** | Abstract social or memory topics |

### Pattern D — Boundary of the known

| | |
|--|--|
| **Purpose** | Cosmic humility + gravity of the question |
| **Psychology** | Awe without scream-cut spectacle |
| **When to use** | Space / deep unknowns |
| **Example** | “There is a boundary in space where even light can't turn around.” (GS-005) |
| **When NOT to use** | Settled classroom physics that needs clarity, not mystery |

### Pattern E — Incomplete famous story

| | |
|--|--|
| **Purpose** | Signal that popular knowledge is missing a piece |
| **Psychology** | Respectful correction of a cultural narrative |
| **When to use** | Extinction, history, “everyone knows” topics |
| **Example** | “Sixty-six million years ago, a rock from space rewrote the story of life on Earth.” → then *not* blast-only (GS-008) |
| **When NOT to use** | When you lack a true missing piece (don’t invent incompleteness) |

### Pattern F — Direct experiential question

| | |
|--|--|
| **Purpose** | Clean promise; immediate topic lock |
| **Psychology** | Shared childhood / adult puzzle |
| **When to use** | Classic “why is…” science (sky, Wi‑Fi, magnets) |
| **Example** | Sky-blue / Wi‑Fi openings that put the phenomenon in the room immediately (GS-003, GS-004) |
| **When NOT to use** | When a quieter personal hook would land harder (see GS-010) |

### Hook rules

1. Original wording every time.  
2. Honest promise within five seconds.  
3. No misleading setups you cannot pay off.  
4. Prefer calm intensity over volume.

|--------------|--------------|-----|
| Thumbnail lie | Trust debt on line one | Promise only what you pay |

| Fake personal (“Hey guys”) | False intimacy | Earn intimacy through recognition |

## 7. Explanation Rules

### One idea · One takeaway

If you cannot state the takeaway in one sentence, the script is not ready.

Examples of Gold takeaways:

| Script | Single takeaway |
|--------|-----------------|
| GS-005 | Science describes the edge of a black hole well; the ultimate interior remains open |
| GS-006 | Yawning probably helps regulate brain state or temperature—but no single explanation is proven |
| GS-009 | Blinking protects and refreshes the eyes while the brain helps visual experience feel continuous |
| GS-010 | Felt time depends on how the brain processes and stores experience—not on time itself changing |

### Spoken English

- Short sentences.
- Natural rhythm.
- Concrete verbs.
- One idea per sentence when possible.
- Read aloud. If you stumble, rewrite.

### Language constraints

| Do | Don’t |
|----|-------|
| Everyday words | Jargon without translation |
| “Dust and haze blocked sunlight” | Geological acronym dumps |
| “Tiny magnetic regions called domains” | Maxwell equations |
| “Several mechanisms likely contribute” | Fake single-cause certainty |

### Never overload

A Short is not a syllabus.  
If a second idea is fascinating, save it for another script.

## 8. The Twist

### What a Curionex twist is

A Curionex twist is the moment the viewer’s mental model upgrades.

It is **not** a jump scare.  
It is **not** “gotcha” humiliation.  
It is the clean turn from *what seemed true* to *what is more true*.

### Three twist types

| Type | Definition | Founding example |
|------|------------|------------------|
| **Plot twist** | Rare for us. Story surprise without science at the center | Generally avoid |
| **Scientific twist** | Evidence reframes a mechanism or myth | Oxygen does not simply cause yawns (GS-006); magnets aren’t gravity (GS-007) |
| **Perspective twist** | The meaning of the phenomenon changes | Birds are living dinosaurs (GS-008); the clock didn’t change—experience did (GS-010) |

Most Gold scripts use a **scientific twist** that enables a **perspective twist**.

### Twists from the Founding Collection

| # | Twist (essence) |
|---|-----------------|
| GS-001 | Infantile amnesia is about how memory systems develop—not a blank baby mind as folklore suggests |
| GS-002 | Dreams remain partly mysterious; honesty is part of the wonder |
| GS-003 | Blue sky is selective scattering—not “the sky is painted blue” |
| GS-004 | Wi‑Fi is radio coordination in shared air—not magic invisible wires |
| GS-005 | We know the doorway; we do not know the core |
| GS-006 | One of our most common behaviors still lacks one accepted explanation |
| GS-007 | A magnet isn’t magic—billions of tiny magnetic regions working together |
| GS-008 | Dinosaurs never completely disappeared |
| GS-009 | The brain dials down awareness during blinks so vision feels continuous |
| GS-010 | Time itself did not change; the way we experience life may have |

### Twist rules

1. Earn it with prior explanation.  
2. Prefer humility that increases wonder.  
3. Never twist into unsupported speculation.

## 9. Perspective Shift

> **This is the most important chapter in the Bible.**

Every Curionex script must permanently change how viewers see something ordinary or familiar.

If the ending only restates the explanation, the script failed—even if every fact is correct.

### Why summaries are forbidden

Summaries say: *Remember what I told you.*  
Perspective shifts say: *You will never see this the same way.*

Summaries shrink the experience back to information.  
Perspective shifts expand it into a new relationship with the world.

### Founding Collection endings — why they work

| # | Ending essence | Why it works |
|---|----------------|--------------|
| GS-001 | Memory’s beginning is itself a kind of mystery | Reframes identity and childhood |
| GS-002 | Dreaming remains an open scientific question | Wonder without fake closure |
| GS-003 | Everyday sky becomes physics you can feel | Ordinary world upgraded |
| GS-004 | Invisible coordination becomes tangible | Demystifies without deflating |
| GS-005 | We’ve mapped the edge of the unknown | Cosmic humility |
| GS-006 | You’ve yawned thousands of times—science still uncovering why | Everyday unfinished science |
| GS-007 | Invisible fields connecting through space | Magnets become more amazing *because* understood |
| GS-008 | The next bird is still singing after Earth’s catastrophe | Survival wonder; emotional permanence |
| GS-009 | Your brain quietly helps you see every second | Intimate partnership with your own mind |
| GS-010 | Time itself did not change. Maybe the way you experience life did. | Signature reflective close of Chapter One |

### Perspective Shift checklist

- [ ] Does the final image live outside the video (window bird, sky, yawn, year)?  
- [ ] Would the viewer tell a friend the *reframe*, not just the *fact*?  
- [ ] Is emotion earned by science—not by soundtrack manipulation in the script?  
- [ ] Did we avoid advice, morals, and motivational quotes?

### The “window test”

After the script ends, imagine the viewer looking up from their phone.

- GS-008: they hear a bird  
- GS-003: they see the sky  
- GS-009: they notice a blink  
- GS-010: they feel a year  

If there is no afterimage in ordinary life, the perspective shift is incomplete.

### Forbidden endings

| Ending type | Why banned |
|-------------|------------|
| Bullet summary | Shrinks wonder to notes |
| “And that’s why…” lecture close | Teacher voice, not Curionex |
| Motivational imperative | Self-help brand break |
| Fake cliffhanger for Part 2 | Manipulation |
| Joke that undercuts the science | Undermines trust |

## 10. Scientific Integrity

Related: [009-editorial-principles.md](./009-editorial-principles.md)

### The four layers (use explicitly in Knowledge Packs)

| Layer | Meaning | VO language |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **Established evidence** | Strong consensus / repeated observation | “Scientists know…” / clear factual voice |
| **Supporting evidence** | Credible but not the whole story | “Evidence suggests…” |
| **Leading hypotheses** | Plausible, actively studied | “One leading idea…” |
| **Unknowns / open questions** | Unresolved | “Still an open question…” / “No single theory has closed the case.” |

### Hard rules

1. **Never invent statistics, quotes, or citations.**  
2. **Never fake certainty** where the literature is open.  
3. **Never exaggerate** for retention.  
4. **Distinguish observation from theory.**  
5. **Prefer primary / authoritative sources:** NASA, ESA, NIH, Nature, Science, Smithsonian, APS, university labs, peer-reviewed reviews.  
6. **Flag claims requiring verification** in Briefs and Spines.  
7. **Correct myths naturally**—mid-flow, not as a scolding PSA.  
8. **Do not use movies, memes, or vibes as evidence.**

### Uncertainty is a feature

GS-005 (black holes), GS-006 (yawns), and GS-002 (dreams) prove that unfinished science can be *more* compelling than false closure—when handled with calm honesty.

### Integrity patterns from the Founding Collection

| Pattern | Script | Lesson |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Known edge / unknown core | GS-005 | Confidence and humility can coexist |
| Myth kill mid-flow | GS-006, GS-007 | Correct without lecturing |
| Multiple functions | GS-009 | Refuse single-cause laziness |
| Several mechanisms | GS-010 | Refuse fake winners |
| Survival without spectacle | GS-008 | Emotion from truth, not fireballs |
| Careful social claim | GS-006 contagion/empathy | Association ≠ proof |

### Fact-check gate

No script ships without:

- Source notes in the Knowledge Pack  
- Explicit myth list (even if short)  
- Human review of any number, name, or causal claim

### Source preference (Curionex)

| Prefer | Avoid as primary |
|--------|------------------|
| NASA, ESA, ESO, USGS | Random blogs |
| NIH, AAO, APS | Viral threads |
| Nature, Science, Current Biology | Undated listicles |
| Smithsonian, university explainers (cross-checked) | Movie physics |
| Named peer-reviewed reviews | Invented citations |

## 11. Writing Rules

| Rule | Spec |
|------|------|
| **Spoken length** | **150–170** words |
| **Duration** | **55–60** seconds |
| **Ideas** | One memorable idea |
| **Sentences** | Short; ear-first |
| **Jargon** | Avoid; translate if essential |
| **Emotion** | Earned; never fake |
| **Filler** | Forbidden |
| **Clickbait** | Forbidden |
| **Manipulation** | Forbidden |
| **Self-help** | Forbidden |
| **Equations** | Forbidden in VO for Shorts |
| **CTA** | Soft; brand-aligned |

### Defaults (Content Standard)

- Duration target: **60** seconds  
- Word target: **~160**

### Editorial hygiene

- Read the Master Script aloud at speaking pace.  
- Cut any line that does not advance Hook → Explanation → Twist → Shift.  
- If a sentence exists only to sound “smart,” delete it.

### Line-level anti-patterns

| Line type | Example smell | Action |
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| Filler bridge | “Now let’s dive in…” | Delete |
| Fake awe | “Insane, right?!” | Rewrite calm |
| Hedge pile | “might possibly perhaps” | One honest hedge |
| Jargon flex | Untranslated acronyms | Translate or cut |
| Summary close | “So to sum up…” | Replace with perspective shift |
| Advice close | “So get out there and…” | Forbidden |
| Certainty cosplay | “Scientists now know for sure that dreams exist to…” | Downgrade to evidence layer |

### Word budget guidance

| Beat | Approx. share of 160 words |
|------|----------------------------|
| Hook + setup | 20–30 |
| Explanation | 60–80 |
| Twist | 25–35 |
| Perspective + CTA | 25–35 |

Budgets flex by topic; the ending must never be starved.

## 12. Quality Review

Editors and AI quality review share one philosophy. Platform checklist lives on the Content Standard; this chapter is the Founding Collection scoring bible.

Related: [010-quality-checklist.md](./010-quality-checklist.md)

### Criteria

| Criterion | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| **Hook** | Does the first breath create honest curiosity? |
| **Retention** | Does each beat advance without dead air? |
| **Curiosity** | Does the viewer need the next line? |
| **Scientific Accuracy** | Are claims layered correctly (evidence / hypothesis / unknown)? |
| **Narrative Flow** | Does structure feel inevitable, not assembled? |
| **Brand Voice** | Friendly, calm, curious, trustworthy? |
| **Emotional Impact** | Is feeling earned by understanding? |
| **Viewer Payoff** | Does the ending change how they see something? |
| **Overall** | Would this sit in the Founding Collection? |

### Thresholds

| Tier | Overall | Rule |
|------|---------|------|
| **Not ready** | &lt; 90 in any critical category, or overall &lt; 90 | Revise |
| **Gold** | Overall **95+**; no category below **90** | Production-ready |
| **Platinum** | Overall **96+**; emotional + integrity exceptional; defines Curionex | Reference-grade |

### Revision policy

- Maximum **3** editorial iterations for a Gold attempt before escalation.  
- If accuracy &lt; 90, stop polishing style—fix science first.  
- If payoff &lt; 90, rewrite the ending before touching the hook.

## 13. Gold vs Platinum

### Gold

**Production ready.**  
Meets voice, structure, integrity, length, and payoff standards. Can publish with confidence among Curionex’s early library.

### Platinum

**Defines Curionex.**  
A Platinum script is not merely correct—it is a teaching artifact. New writers should study it before drafting. It remains meaningful years later. It could open a pitch deck.

### Platinum references (Founding Collection)

| Script | Why Platinum |
|--------|--------------|
| **GS-005 — What Happens Inside a Black Hole?** | Integrity masterpiece: known edge / unknown core without spectacle |
| **GS-008 — Why Did Dinosaurs Go Extinct?** | Emotional permanence without disaster-movie language; bird survival as heart, not trivia |
| **GS-010 — Why Does Time Feel Faster As You Get Older?** | Signature reflective finale; evidence-first emotion; Chapter One close |

### Strong Gold (study also)

GS-001, GS-006, GS-007, GS-009 — exemplary craft; use freely as pattern libraries.

## 14. Editorial Principles

Freeze these as official:

1. **Wonder over sensationalism.**  
2. **Evidence before certainty.**  
3. **Teach one unforgettable idea.**  
4. **Respect audience intelligence.**  
5. **End with perspective—not summary.**  
6. **Scientific honesty over virality.**  
7. **Calm over volume.**  
8. **Separate known, hypothesized, and unknown.**  
9. **Correct myths without humiliating the viewer.**  
10. **Earn emotion; never manipulate it.**  
11. **Short form is a craft constraint, not an excuse for shallowness.**  
12. **Every script must be willing to sit beside the Founding Collection.**

> Pull quote: *If virality requires a lie, we don’t ship.*

## 16. Future Writing

Scripts **#11–#120** (and beyond) are evaluated against one question:

> **Would this belong beside the Founding Collection?**

### Evaluation gate for new scripts

1. Does it obey this Bible and the active Content Standard?  
2. Is there a single takeaway?  
3. Is the ending a perspective shift?  
4. Are evidence layers honest?  
5. Would any Founding Platinum author be proud to sign it?  
6. If we removed the logo, would it still sound like Curionex?

### Production guidance

- Prefer evergreen catalog topics with Priority A / early waves.  
- Use Editorial Library → Project → Knowledge Pack → Brief → Spine → Master → Review.  
- Do not lower the bar to fill a calendar. Silence is better than dilution.

### When to revise the Bible

- New formats (longer videos, series) may require a **v1.1+** addendum.  
- Do not silently drift voice “because Shorts changed.”  
- Platform Content Standard updates should stay consistent with this document—or this document updates in the same change set.

## 17. Appendix — Checklists

### A. Editorial checklist (gate)

- [ ] Topic fit for audience and brand  
- [ ] Knowledge Pack complete (research / facts / sources / angle)  
- [ ] Discovery Brief: question, promise, takeaway, duration  
- [ ] Story Spine: beats + risks + claims to verify  
- [ ] Master Script: 150–170 words; 55–60s  
- [ ] Hook honest within 5 seconds  
- [ ] One idea only  
- [ ] Twist earned  
- [ ] Perspective shift ending (no summary)  
- [ ] Myths corrected naturally  
- [ ] Evidence layers labeled where needed  
- [ ] Quality scores meet Gold threshold  
- [ ] CTA brand-aligned  

### B. Writer checklist

- [ ] I can state the takeaway in one sentence  
- [ ] I read the script aloud at natural pace  
- [ ] No jargon left unexplained  
- [ ] No invented numbers or fake citations  
- [ ] No clickbait hook  
- [ ] No self-help ending  
- [ ] Emotion comes from understanding  
- [ ] I would stand behind this beside GS-010  

### C. Reviewer checklist

- [ ] Score all criteria (Hook → Overall)  
- [ ] Any category &lt; 90 → return for revision  
- [ ] Accuracy issues outrank style polish  
- [ ] Payoff issues require ending rewrite  
- [ ] Confirm sources are real and sufficient  
- [ ] Confirm unknowns are not papered over  
- [ ] Confirm perspective shift is present  

### D. Scientific integrity checklist

- [ ] Established vs hypothesis vs unknown separated  
- [ ] Primary / authoritative sources preferred  
- [ ] No invented statistics  
- [ ] No exaggerated certainty  
- [ ] Myths listed and addressed  
- [ ] Movies / memes not used as evidence  
- [ ] Claims requiring verification flagged  

### E. Publication readiness checklist

- [ ] Gold or Platinum certified  
- [ ] Studio documents saved (Pack + Brief + Spine + Master)  
- [ ] Title matches promise  
- [ ] Visual notes do not require false imagery  
- [ ] No legal/medical advice landmines  
- [ ] Final read-aloud pass completed  
- [ ] Founding Collection test passed
